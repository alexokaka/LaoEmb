Visa & Consular Information

Everything you need to know about applying for a visa to visit the Lao PDR from Australia and New Zealand.

📋

Visa Requirements

To apply for a Lao visa, you will need:

  • Original passport valid for at least 6 months with 2+ blank pages
  • Completed visa application form with passport-sized photo
  • Copy of travel itinerary
  • Pre-paid self-addressed return envelope (if applying by mail)
  • Visa fees payable by money order or cash

Visa Fees

Current visa processing fees:

  • Standard Processing (5 working days): AU$
  • Urgent Processing (1 working day): AU$
  • Fees include handling charges
  • Payment: Money Order or Cash to "The Lao PDR Embassy"

e-Visa & Visa on Arrival

Alternative options for obtaining a Lao visa:

  • e-Visa: Apply online at laoevisa.gov.la

About the Lao PDR

Explore the beauty, culture, and heritage of the Lao People's Democratic Republic — the Land of a Million Elephants.

🌍

Geography & Climate

  • Landlocked country bordered by China, Vietnam, Cambodia, Thailand and Myanmar
  • Total Area: 236,800 sq km
  • Tropical climate with rainy season from May to October
  • Population density: 30 people per sq km
  • Mountainous terrain traversed by the Mekong River
📊

Key Facts

  • Capital: Vientiane
  • Population: 7.032 million
  • Official Language: Lao (English widely understood in cities)
  • Religion: Theravada Buddhism (~65%)
  • Currency: Lao Kip (LAK)
  • Time Zone: GMT+7 | Country Code: +856
  • Major Cities: Vientiane, Luang Prabang, Savannakhet, Champassak
👥

People & Culture

The Lao PDR is home to a multi-ethnic people and multi-cultural society with a long history of rich variety of cultures and customs. The 50 ethnic groups speak different dialects across 4 main linguistic families:

  • Lao — Tai family
  • Mone — Khmer family
  • Tibeto — Chinese family
  • Hmong — Loumien family
🛕

Religion & Language

The original belief of the Lao multi-ethnic people was animism, which was later influenced by Indian culture. Brahmanism came next, followed by Buddhism. Today about 65% of the Lao people are Theravada Buddhists, though animism continues to co-exist with Buddhism.

The Lao national language is a monosyllable-based dialect. Except for words influenced by Pali or Sanskrit (Indian origin), the Lao letters are derived from Pali characters.

📜

Ancient Civilisation & Lane Xang Kingdom

Stone tools and human traces discovered across the country — especially in Houaphan and Luang Prabang provinces — evidence prehistoric man in Lao territory at least 40,000 years ago.

In 1353, King Fa Ngum unified the scattered Lao principalities into the Kingdom of Lane Xang — "the Land of a Million Elephants." During the 16th century, King Saya Setthathirat moved the capital to Vientiane and constructed the iconic Ho Phakeo temple and That Luang stupa. King Souriyavongsa's 57-year reign (from 1638) is regarded as the nation's golden age, when influence extended to Yunnan (China), the Shan States, Northeastern Thailand, and parts of Vietnam and Cambodia.

🏛️

Modern History & Independence

After King Souriyavongsa's death, dynastic struggles divided Lane Xang into three kingdoms: Luang Prabang, Vientiane, and Champassak — each later falling under foreign rule.

In 1893, Luang Prabang sought French protection, and all three kingdoms were placed under the French protectorate. In 1945, the Lao people proclaimed independence from France, later recognised by the United Nations.

On 2 December 1975, the Lao People's Democratic Republic was founded — marking the rebirth of a unified Lao nation. Today, the Lao people enjoy peace, stability, and freedom.

That Luang Stupa

The That Luang Stupa is the most important Buddhist symbol in Laos. Standing 45 metres high and 49 metres wide at its base, it is surrounded by 30 smaller stupas. Constructed in 1566 by skilled technicians of the Lane Xang Kingdom during the reign of King Saya Setthathirat, it was built over an older stupa believed to mark the founding of Vientiane — "the City of Sandalwood."

Luang Prabang

A UNESCO World Heritage Site — "an alive museum of the past." Renowned for well-preserved temples, the sacred alms giving ceremony, Wat Xiengthong, and the serene charm of this ancient royal capital.

Discover More →

Plain of Jars

The Plain of Jars, located on a plateau in central Laos, gets its name from more than 2,100 tubular-shaped megalithic stone jars used for funerary practices in the Iron Age. This serial property of 15 components contains large carved stone jars, stone discs, secondary burials, tombstones, quarries and funerary objects dating from 500 BCE to 500 CE. The jars and associated elements are the most prominent evidence of the Iron Age civilization that made and used them until it disappeared, around 500 CE.

Discover More →

Vientiane Capital

The charming capital blends ancient Buddhist temples with colonial charm. Home to the iconic That Luang golden stupa, the Patuxai Victory Monument (triumphal arch), Wat Sisaket, and the renowned Ho Phakeo temple.

Discover More →

Hin Nam No National Park

Hin Nam No National Park is a natural marvel that spans an area of 94,121 hectares in southeastern Laos. This biodiverse and stunningly beautiful protected zone shelters some of the planet’s most spectacular, mysterious, and highly threatened biodiversity and harbors an array of ecosystems.

Discover More →

Southern Laos

Explore the ruins of Wat Phu Champassak, a UNESCO-listed temple complex built from the 6th to 8th centuries, and the magnificent Khonephapheng Waterfall — the largest waterfall on the Mekong River. Discover the Plain of Jars in Xiengkhouang — mysterious 3,000-year-old stone jars.
